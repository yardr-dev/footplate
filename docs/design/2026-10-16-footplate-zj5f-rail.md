# A first-level rail in footplate: chat, decisions, timetable

Design footplate-zj5f, proposed 2026-10-16. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

Design for bead footplate-zj5f (flow `design.design`, stage `draft`). A yard is one installation of yardr: its beads, its crew and its depots. A bead is one durable piece of work with a stage; a depot is where the bead is worked. footplate is the yard's native app, a window onto one yard that talks to it over the socket API. The crew are the yard's agents; decisions are the crew's open asks put to the person. An arrival is a bead still travelling, shown with when it is expected; the platform is the stage a bead stands at; the line is its depot; the timetable is arrivals and flows together. A flow is the stages a depot's beads travel, in travel order, each stage routed to a group. A lamp is a stage's wait light: fault or turn. See docs/glossary.md in the yardr repository for all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

mckean, 16 Oct 2026 07:59, by mail: "introduce a 1st level nav on the far left similar to Slack with maybe just icons for chat, decisions and timetable, and at the top the icon. Then if selecting chat we get the crew and the chat, when selecting decisions we get the list with the decisions (already expanded, no need to collapse), when clicking on timetable we might want a way to inspect individual flows or see the arrivals. The switcher and settings go to the bottom." How does footplate's yard window become three modes behind a rail, what does each mode show in its two panes, what does the rail cost in width, and in what order is it built.

**The kit has tokens and a panel, no rail and no mode icons.** The yardr-design repository's readme.md is the brand: the railway vocabulary (never "tasks" or "dashboard"), one rust accent, green only for the running dot, one status row (a dot, the state word, a quiet action, nothing else), one shadow, for popovers, counts as quiet words on the right (never a badge), targets at least 32 pixels, icon-only buttons only in the header, always with a label or tooltip. There is no rail component and no segmented control ("the kit has no segmented control", src/window.rs:7885). footplate draws its own glyphs where it needs them (the switcher's chevron is two strokes, src/window.rs:607).

**The away screen keeps the head and drops the rows.** `away()` (src/window.rs:7516) is the full right pane while the yard is away: the icon, the why, the details, the ways to a yard. Beside it the window keeps the column with its head and none of its rows, so the person switches yards from where they always do; the one environment-named yard keeps no column at all (src/window.rs:10172-10179). The settings button stands under the away screen's words where there is no column (`settings_control`, src/window.rs:6123).

**The phone is parked.** footplate-fwfq (a footplate on a phone) waits behind the hosted trial; no native phone footplate is decided.

## 3. The proposal

The rail stands on the far left of the yard window, 56 pixels wide: three mode icons under the footplate mark at the top, the switcher and settings at the foot. Each mode is the window as today with one list and one pane: chat is the crew and the conversation, decisions is the expanded list and the card, timetable is the lines and the board or a flow. The window's floor grows by the rail's width, to 616 by 360, so no pane shrinks. The mock (docs/design/2026-10-16-footplate-zj5f-rail-mock.html, with its twelve screenshots: three modes at 616 by 360 and at 1200 by 800, dark and light) draws every frame below from the kit's tokens.

### 3.1. The rail

**Width: 56 pixels, fixed.** The kit's minimum target is 32 pixels and its sidebar side padding is 12: 12 + 32 + 12 is 56. It is fixed pixels, not rems: the rail carries no reading text (icons and two letters), so it need not grow with the text setting, and a fixed width keeps the floor's arithmetic exact. What it costs is only width: 56 pixels off every window, given back by the wider floor.

**At the top the then-current footplate mark, doing nothing.** At the time of this note, the kit's track glyph on a graphite tile was the small app icon: two rails, three sleepers. The wye Y from yardr's style guide replaced it on 7 Nov 2026 (see [The icon](../building.md#the-icon)); the track glyph remains the timetable mode icon. It did nothing on click or key. The reasons: yards have names, not marks, so the top cannot name the yard; mckean puts the switcher at the foot, and two switchers would ask which one switches; the mark is orientation, as the logo in today's head is. Its tooltip says "footplate".

**The three mode icons, chat, decisions, timetable from top to bottom**, in mckean's order. The kit has no railway icon family, so the three are drawn for footplate in the track glyph's stroke (2-pixel strokes, current color, 16 pixels), and offered back to the kit: chat is a signal lamp on its post (a circle over a short post): the crew signals, and the lamp is the signal that talks; decisions is a semaphore signal arm, diagonal on its post (stop or go, decline or approve); timetable is the kit's track glyph itself. A speech bubble would read faster as chat, and the app illustration even carries a brass one, but brass stays in illustration and icons stay in the railway family (track, locomotive, signals, wagons), so the bubble is refused: the lamp, the arm and the track read apart at 16 pixels. The open mode's icon stands in the accent on the open ground (`palette.open`, as the open row does); the others stand quiet and brighten on hover, as quiet text does. Every one carries a tooltip with its name and keys, by the kit's icon-only rule.

**At the foot the switcher and the settings.** The switcher is a tile, 32 pixels, rounded, in `border-strong`: the open yard's first two letters, in the section type. The full name stands in the list it opens, in the settings, and on every card header, as today. The tile answers the design's hardest constraint, 5wz5's "the head always names the open yard": the rail removes the head, so the tile names it instead, always, in two letters where the head used twenty. Its tooltip is the whole telling ("mac — switch yards (⌘Y)"). Click or `secondary-y` opens the yard list as a popover to the right of the rail's foot, in the kit's popover shadow, with today's rows (name, kind, state, last-known counts) and today's keys (up and down, Enter, Escape, ⌘1–⌘9): a 56-pixel rail leaves no room for names, and the popover is the kit's mechanism for exactly that. The settings is the kit's gear below the tile, today's toggle on click and `secondary-,`, lit while the settings stand where the right pane does. With the one environment-named yard there is no tile, as today there is no switcher: the mark, the modes, the gear.

**Between modes: `secondary-[` and `secondary-]` step from anywhere**, wrapping around the three, or a click. The bindings have no context, as the switcher's keys do, so they answer with the focus in the line too. The reasons: `secondary` with digits is the yards', plain keys are the cards', Tab steps rows; the brackets are free, sit together, and read as the tab strip the modes are. There are no direct per-mode keys: two presses reach any mode, and three more bindings would spend keys the cards may want.

**The mode is remembered per yard, in the settings**, as a map from yard name to mode, default chat, left out of the file while every yard is chat: the `arrivals` set's pattern, grown from a boolean to a mode. A restart reopens each yard where the person lived in it. Within a run each mode keeps its own open row and scroll per yard (`Place` grows a row per mode), so leaving chat for the timetable and coming back finds the conversation where it stood: without that every mode switch would reset to the top, and the modes would punish looking.

### 3.2. Chat

Chat is the window as today with one list: the status row at the head (`switch_state`, unchanged: the dot, the state word, the quiet Start/Stop), then the crew rows, then the yard row ("yard · notifications") below them, as today. The Decisions section leaves for the decisions mode; the arrivals row lives in the timetable (61yx's row moves there with the mode). The right pane is today's: the conversation, the yard's notices, or "Nobody to write to yet." The yard's notifications row lives here because notifications are mail the yard wrote to the person, and chat is where the person reads mail.

### 3.3. Decisions

The list shows the decisions already expanded: the topic groups as today, and each row with every ask expanded, in ask order: each ask's question first line, then its numbered options with their words. An ask with no numbered options shows what its card shows instead: a secret ask the yard's line (`secret.line`, as the card's `secret_lines` does, src/window.rs:9576), a run ask its command line (`run.line()`, as the card's `run_block` does, src/window.rs:9594), an ask with neither its "words wanted", as the card's words-only case does (src/window.rs:9281-9286). Then the sub line (asker and waits, or the oldest waits where several ask, `decision_sub` in src/model.rs:2695) and the short id. The options come from the same Bead read as the card, so a bead not yet read shows its head alone until the read lands; secret and run asks show their line and command from the queue without waiting. The card whole stays on the right: context, recommendation, the line for words, the keys, expand. The list is for triage, the right is for answering: options in the list take no clicks and no keys, so there is exactly one place a pick happens, and the keys 1–9 keep meaning the open card. The Decided toggle stays at the list's end: decided asks are unbounded history, and without the toggle they would push open decisions off a 360-pixel list. "Expanded" at the floor height means the rows are taller and the list scrolls, on the same scroll the crew list has always had: one single-ask row stands whole with the next row's head under it, and a bead with several asks takes what height its asks need.

### 3.4. Timetable

**The list is the arrivals row, the lines, and No line.** First the arrivals row, "arrivals · M in transit", with M the yard-wide travelling total the Flows call serves; until the Flows call lands, 61yx's row stands as it is, its count from the served rows. Then one row per line the Flows call serves, each with its travelling total ("yardr · 5 in transit", "footplate · nothing in transit"). Every served line stands here, travelling or not: the flows are for inspection too, and an empty line's stages and routes are worth seeing. The lines stand in the order the yard serves them; footplate sorts nothing, by the arrivals no-logic rule. Below the lines, only while beads travel with no line, stands a No line row ("No line · N in transit"): a bead with no depot has no line's flow to stand in (it stands at new under no flow, yardr internal/store/asks.go:515-518), so it stands here instead. It stands last, and only when N is above none: unlike a line, it has no stages worth inspecting empty. The mock shows a yard with none travelling without a line, so its timetable lists show no No line row. The status row heads the list, as in every mode.

**The right pane is the board, a flow, No line, or a bead's details.** The arrivals row shows 61yx's board pane as it is, under the complete total ("M in transit · all lines"): the served rows grouped by platform, in the served platforms order once yardr-8w9z lands, at most 15 rows; where more travel, a quiet last line reads "15 of M shown". A line row shows that line's flows: each flow a section (flow name, its type where typed, its source), each flow its stages in travel order, each stage its name, its count, its lamp, and its beads beneath (time in mono, title, faint id, remark in its tone: the board's rows, drawn again). The lamp is red for fault, rust for turn, nothing where neither burns: the web's two lamps in the palette footplate has, where the web's turn lamp is blue and footplate has no blue, so the turn lamp takes the kit's one rust accent, the waiting color. A bead's remark tone on the wire stays the Arrivals words (`red`, `amber`, `none`); footplate draws `amber` in the same rust. Stages never collapse: mckean's expanded rule extends from decisions to flows. With open question 3's recommended option, an Elsewhere section stands under the flows, as on /flows. The No line row shows its beads as a plain list, newest change first, each row drawn as a flow bead (time in mono, title, faint id, remark in its tone): no flows, no stages, no lamps, because a bead with no depot travels no flow. A bead's click, on the board, on a flow, or under No line, shows 61yx's details pane: title, id, platform, line ("no line" for a bead with none), expected arrival and remark from the row it came from, labels, body, notes, the Open-the-page action where the web view's address is set, the asks line ("2 questions, among your decisions", linking nothing), and at the top the quiet back link naming where it came from ("arrivals", the line's name, or "no line"). A person moves from the list to the board, a line, or No line where it stands, from any of those to a bead, and from the bead back.

**What the yard must serve: a `Flows` call in the Arrivals pattern.** One call, no arguments, served once and drawn without computing:

```text
POST /call/Flows
{}
```

Its result is the yard-wide travelling total, the yard's depots (each with its travelling total, its flows, each flow its stages in travel order with the open beads on each and its lamp, and, with open question 3's recommended option, its Elsewhere rows), and the beads travelling with no line:

```text
{"travelling": 7,
 "depots": [
   {"name": "yardr", "kind": "repo",
    "travelling": 5,
    "flows": [
      {"name": "default", "type": "", "source": "repo@0f415ee",
       "stages": [
         {"stage": "new", "group": "muse-builders", "human": false,
          "lamp": "turn",
          "beads": [
            {"bead": "yardr-bwm2", "title": "…",
             "expected": "2h–3h", "remark": "waits for mckean",
             "tone": "amber", "working": true}]}]}],
    "elsewhere": []}],
 "unassigned": []}
```

It reuses three computations that exist: the /flows builder (yardr internal/web/beads.go) for depots, flows, stages in order and the beads on each; `panelMarks` (yardr internal/web/panel.go) for the lamps; the Arrivals row computation for each bead's expected arrival, remark and tone, for the flow beads and the unassigned rows alike, so "expected" keeps its one definition and the board and the flows can never disagree about a bead. It is uncapped: a flow view that hides beads misleads, and open beads number in tens, not thousands. The `travelling` totals count open beads outside the backlog: each depot's counts its own, the yard-wide counts every one, and `unassigned` carries the open beads outside the backlog with no depot, newest change first, each row shaped as a flow bead. The yard-wide total is the depots' totals plus the unassigned count, so the list's counts are complete where the board's 15 rows are not; with open question 3's recommended option every counted bead is drawn, in some line's flow or Elsewhere, or under No line. It is in `Calls` with its entry in docs/api.md, served on the local socket and to a paired device alike. footplate re-reads it on the Arrivals invalidating set plus one: every event that carries a bead (moves, holds, notes, sessions), and the events without one that move its inputs (`group_set` and `group_removed`, `route_added` and `route_removed`, `flow_set`, `flow_removed`, `flow_pointer_set` and `flow_pointer_cleared`, `depot_updated`, `depot_removed`, and `setting_changed` for `max_sessions` and `operator`). The plus one is the price of listing every line: `DeleteDepot` refuses while open beads remain (yardr internal/store/graph.go:1279), so the Arrivals set never needs it, but a removed depot's line must leave the timetable, and only `depot_removed` (graph.go:1343) says so. A new depot arrives as `depot_updated` (`PutDepot` adds or replaces, graph.go:1218), which the set already has.

### 3.5. The floor

**560 grows by the rail: the floor is 616 by 360.** The yardmaster's view, and the proposal takes it: every pane keeps its size (the list 270, chat's right pane 290, as today), and the tighter alternative, 214-pixel rows, would cost more in wrapping and reflow than 56 pixels of floor. The change is one line in src/main.rs:112. The status-row test becomes one test per mode at 616 by 360: in chat, decisions and timetable the status row holds one line with its action inside the list's edge (the "which yard" anchor is gone with the head; the tests anchor on the list's edge instead). A rail test pins the width (56), the order (chat, decisions, timetable), the inert top mark, and the foot's tile and gear.

### 3.6. The away screen and the phone

**The rail stays while the yard is away**, as the head with the switcher stays today: the mark at the top, the three mode icons dimmed and inert (their tooltips say the yard is away), the foot's switcher and settings alive, because leaving an away yard is the foot's job. The away pane stands beside the rail as today, with its details and its ways to a yard; so does the first run. The one environment-named yard keeps its rail with the gear and no tile, which retires the settings button under the away screen's words. footplate-fwfq stays parked; what the rail leaves open for it is a bottom tab bar with the same three modes behind it, the per-yard mode memory reused as is. That is all this design says about the phone.

### 3.7. At the floor, in each mode

At 616 by 360 a person in chat sees the rail (mark, three icons, yard tile, gear), the status row, three crew rows and the yard row, and beside them the open conversation's head with its newest mail and the line to type in; in decisions the rail, the status row, one expanded decision whole with its options and the next row's head, and beside them the open card with its options, words line and keys; in timetable the rail, the status row, the arrivals row and two lines, and beside them the board's first platform with its beads, or the open line's first flow with its stages, lamps and beads (No line stands below the lines only while beads travel with none).

## 4. Left out

**Words under the rail's icons at wide windows.** mckean asked for just icons; labels are a second layout with their own widths and wraps, for a gain a tooltip already gives. Open question 1 keeps it.

**Direct per-mode keys.** Two presses of the brackets reach any mode; three more bindings would spend keys the cards may want. They return if living with stepping chafes.

**Clickable options in the decisions list.** Answering stays in the right card, so the keys 1–9 keep one meaning and there is one place a pick happens. The list triages; the card decides.

**Collapsing anything but Decided history.** mckean: already expanded, no need to collapse. Stages, groups and rows stand open; only the unbounded decided past keeps its toggle. (The Waiting section that came after this note folds too, as of footplate-c34r, 10 Nov 2026: a long queue of parked beads pushed the open decisions away. The open decisions, their topic groups, stages and groups still stand open.)

**A per-depot Flows call.** One call serves the whole yard, as Arrivals does: one re-read, one invalidating set, no call per line and no partial states.

**A cap on the flows.** Arrivals caps at 15 rows; the flows serve every open bead, because a flow view that hides beads misleads. The cost is a larger result for a large yard, in tens of rows.

**Per-bead dependency edges.** The web panel marks which bead waits on which (`dep` in `panelMark`); the served bead rows carry no edge, and the invalidating set needs no dependency events. The remark still says held, stalled, waiting or late. Edges return as one field when a flow view asks for them.

**signalbox reading the Flows call.** Like the Arrivals view before it (8v5t section 4), the served view is footplate's; signalbox reads the web view's JSON, not the socket, and stays as it is.

**Moving a bead from the timetable.** No move, hold or answer from the board, the flows or the details: 61yx's rule stands, and the asks line links nothing. Actions live on the cards.

**One list of every yard's rows.** 5wz5 refused it, and the rail changes nothing: the tile always names the open yard, and nothing goes from one yard to another.

**The Elsewhere beads.** Whether a line's flow view carries its off-the-line beads is open question 3. The beads with no line are not left out with them: `unassigned` is served and drawn unconditionally under No line, because without it the yard-wide total would count beads no flow view shows.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Whether the rail gains words at wide windows.** Option A, icons always, as proposed: one layout, tooltips carry the names, mckean's "just icons" as asked. Option B, labels under the icons past some width (1000 pixels, say): the modes name themselves without hovering, at the cost of a second layout, wider rails, and wrapped words in two-letter tiles. I would take A: the rail is three icons a person learns in a day, and the tooltip is already designed.
2. **Whether the details pane's asks line links to the decisions mode.** Option A, plain words as 61yx has it ("2 questions, among your decisions", linking nothing): no cross-mode mechanism, and the person switches modes by hand. Option B, the line jumps to the decisions mode with that bead's card open: one click from a bead to its questions, at the cost of the first cross-mode link, with its own keys and its own back behavior. I would take A: one link does not pay for a mechanism, and the second link, if it comes, designs it properly.
3. **Whether a line's flow view carries its Elsewhere beads.** Option A, flows only: the view is stages with beads and lamps; a bead in no stage of the line's flows (a pinned flow of its own, a changed flow's leftover) stays findable in the CLI, and on the arrivals board only while it is among the 15 served rows; and the line's travelling total still counts it, so the count can read more than the view draws. (Beads with no line are unaffected: `unassigned` is served and drawn under No line either way.) Option B, an Elsewhere section under the flows, as /flows has it: every counted bead is drawn, in some line's flow or Elsewhere, or under No line, at the cost of one more section per line and the served rows for it. I would take B: a timetable that cannot show every travelling bead is a board with trains missing.

## 6. The beads it would become

Three beads, in the order they land. The second can land before the first: the rail ships with the timetable carried over from 61yx, and the flows arrive last. Every footplate bead touches src/window.rs, so the two footplate beads chain one at a time.
