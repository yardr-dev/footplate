# The beads waiting in a stage you move, in Decide, with their moves

Design footplate-7k20, proposed 2026-11-09. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

Design for bead footplate-7k20 (flow `design.design`, stage `draft`). A yard is one installation of yardr: its beads, its crew and its depots. A bead is one durable piece of work with a stage; a depot is where the bead is worked; a flow is the stages a depot's beads travel, in travel order, each stage routed to a group; a manual stage is a stage routed to a group of people; an ask is one question as data on a bead; a stage ask is the ask a stage's `## Ask` template puts on a bead that enters the stage (yardr `docs/flows.md`, "The ask template"); parking is standing in a manual stage with nothing running and nothing asked that moves the bead. footplate is the yard's native window, speaking the socket calls yardr's `docs/api.md` promises. Decide is the window's decisions mode (`Mode::Decisions` in `src/model.rs`, the rail's "Decide"); Lines is its timetable mode. See `docs/glossary.md` in the yardr repository for all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

mckean, reporting a new user's screenshot (mail 165298): "it is waiting for me to do actions but there is no place to do the action except command line... one of the biggest usability blockers especially for new users". On the shape (mail 165305) he offered two: a section of its own in Decide, or a "mine"/"assigned to me" filter on the Lines view whose bead detail offers the possible actions. The yardmaster recommends a section of its own in Decide, because the complaint is that the person has to keep track of where to act, and two places put that back; the Lines bead detail gains the same moves as a second step, for a person who finds the bead there first. Which beads that wait on a person stand in footplate, where they stand, and with what moves, so that no bead waits only at a terminal?

## 2. What exists today

**Decide lists asks alone.** The decisions list (`list_decisions` in `src/window/asks.rs`) draws the open asks grouped by topic from `UserAsks` (`OpenAsk`, `Asks` in `src/api.rs`; `Model::decision_groups` in `src/model.rs`), then the folded Decided history, then, for the operator's window, the Grants, Teammates and Pair-a-phone rows (`order_in` for `Mode::Decisions` in `src/model.rs`). A bead enters that list only by carrying an open ask addressed to the reader. The bead this design is about — parked in a manual stage such as `ready` or `siding`, or a `production-approval` stage a depot's flow names, with no ask on it — has no row, no card and no count: `decisions_count` counts beads with open asks, and `Counts` in `src/window/state.rs` sums that with unread mail for the rail, the switcher and the dock. Memory bead mem-xyzn records the same gap from the other side: a design bead sitting in `decide` was invisible here until its questions were written as asks.

**A stage ask already makes a Decide card that moves the bead, and this design must not show that bead twice.** A stage file ending in `## Ask` puts one ask per user-member of the stage's group when a bead enters the stage, and the member's pick moves the bead in the same write (yardr `docs/flows.md`:563 and the pages after it; depot yardr's `decide` stages carry one, the starter pack's does not, and `backlog` and the sidings carry none by design, where beads wait for company). footplate already draws those asks as ordinary Decide cards and answers them with `AnswerAsk` (`src/api.rs`), so a templated stage needs nothing here. The gap is exactly the manual stages without a template, and any parked bead whose open asks — where it has any — do not move it. The de-duplication rule in section 3.1 is keyed on that: while a bead carries an open ask to the reader it stands in Decisions alone, and it joins the new list only when no open ask to the reader stands on it.

**`yardr review` is the queue this mirrors, and it is terminal-only.** `yardr review` walks the open beads in stages routed to manual groups — only groups that list the reader as a member, where they list members — longest untouched first, showing stage, description, last notes and `git diff --stat` (yardr `docs/review.md`; `reviewQueue` in yardr `internal/cli/review.go`). `yardr review list --json` carries the same queue with its moves (`reviewItem`, `reviewAction` there): for each bead, the outcomes its flow names for the stage, read for that bead so a round limit can send the report elsewhere, as `done` actions; where the flow names no outcomes, the stages it allows, as `advance` actions; a return to the stage whose question brought the bead, where there is one; and for every move a label in the flow's words ("done: move to approved", "send back to new (note required)", "finish as merged (closes the bead)"), a `note_required` flag where the move goes back to an earlier stage, and a `terminal` flag where entering the stage closes the bead. A bead whose flow does not resolve carries a `flow_error` and offers no move; one whose stage template broke carries a `template_error` where the ask would stand, and still moves by hand. Review includes the backlog — its help says "backlog is manual too", and its `-g` flag narrows by group. The two moves a person makes there are `yardr bead done <id> --outcome <o> [--note …]` and `yardr bead advance <id> --to <stage> [--note …]`, both refusing a stale bead: the store's `Done` and `AdvanceNote` (yardr `internal/store/beads.go`) take an `Expect` of the stage and revision the move was decided from, add the note in the same write where one is given, and refuse where the flow allows no such move.

**The web view already acts on the parked beads, under a different name for the same rule.** The yard's web Home carries `attention` (yardr `docs/web.md`:847-855; `attention` in `internal/web/home.go`): everything something stops, the beads with an open ask to the reader, and the parked beads past their flow's first whose every manual wait the reader has not answered, faults first. The parked half is computed in `waits` (`internal/web/waits.go`): an open bead whose only reasons are `manual` ones — its working group, or the group its routes send it to, is a manual group — stands in `Parked`; a member of several who has decided every ask to them on the bead, with another member left, gets the words "you answered; waits for …" and Home leaves the bead out of its count (`AnsweredOnly`). On a view that acts, a bead's page offers the same moves review offers, with a note, from the page's revision (`moveActions` and `beadMove` in `internal/web/acts.go`, writing through the same store `Done`/`AdvanceNote` with `Expect{Stage, Revision}`); a stale page is read again with why, never written over. On a phone the web rows already give an asked question its first line whole, with the bead id, topic and age under it. That is the phone surface that exists today; this design changes nothing there.

**No stable socket call reads that queue, and none moves a bead.** The calls a program that is not yardr may rely on are `Calls` and `OperatorCalls` (yardr `internal/rpc/api.go`, held to `docs/api.md` by its test). They hold `UserAsks`, `Bead` and the ask writes (`AnswerAsk`, `DeferAsk`, `DeclineAsk`, `GiveSecret`, `ApproveAsk`, `ApproveSetup`) — and no `Done`, `Advance` or move of any name: those store methods exist on the socket only in yardr's own array form, classed for a bead's agent in its pane (`Advance`, `AdvanceNote`, `Done` as `Agent` in `internal/rpc/access.go`), which no device is served and no client may rely on. `NextStages` — where a bead may go, with its outcomes read for the bead, its allowed stages, its return stage and its template error (`NextStages` in `internal/store/flows.go`) — is likewise yardr's own: classed `Read` on the socket, absent from `Calls`. footplate's `Bead` answer (`Bead` in `src/api.rs`: body, stage, labels, notes, asks) carries no revision and no next move; its `moves` names in yardr's `Bead` are the bead's recorded history, not where it may go. So with yardr as it stands, footplate can neither list the parked beads nor move one, however it draws them. Section 3.4 names the two calls it needs; both are new, and small because the store already computes everything they serve.

**What footplate already reads per yard carries the shape, but not the fact.** Every yard the window follows is read whole by `session::load` (`src/session.rs`) into a `State` — who the window is for, the asks, the arrivals, the flows, the operator's rows — and followed on its own thread; a yard that is not open stands as an aside model (`Held` in `src/window/state.rs`) whose `Counts` keep its switcher row and the dock's sum current. Inside the open yard, `Flows` (yardr's call; `FlowStage` in `src/api.rs` with its `group`, `human`, `lamp`, seats, and `FlowBead` with its remark and tone) already places every travelling bead on its stage and says which stages wait on a person — but it serves no group's members, no bead's last change, and no moves, and the backlog's beads stand on their stage in it while its travelling total leaves them out. footplate's own "waiting on you" for the Lines panel (`Model::waiting_on_you` in `src/model.rs`, drawn by `panel_summary` and `waits_banner` in `src/window/arrivals.rs`) is remark-based: a bead counts where its served remark names the reader alone, or where the reader has an open ask on it. A bead parked for a group of several therefore counts there only once it is asked — the same undercount this design removes in Decide, and section 3.5 makes the two counts one.

**How footplate asks and answers today.** Reads ride `Effect` and `Read` (`src/model.rs`): the model answers an effect, `Footplate::run_in` (`src/window/chrome.rs`) performs it by the yard's client, answers come back stamped and an older answer of a kind is dropped (`Reads`), and a failed read is made again after a while (`docs/yards.md`). `UserAsks` is re-read on every `noted` event (the yard's `docs/api.md` says so too); arrivals and flows are re-read on every event about a bead and on the group, route, flow and depot changes named with those calls (`Model::event` in `src/model.rs`). Writes — `AnswerAsk`, `GiveSecret`, `ApproveAsk` and the rest — are made from the cards in `src/window/asks.rs` by the client directly, each with the window's user as actor; a write that times out may have run, is never asked again, and the card says the yard did not answer and reads again (`Error::may_have_run` in `src/api.rs`; the timeout rule `READS` encodes, held to the tables of `docs/yards.md` by a test there). The stand-in yard (`Yard` in `src/fake.rs`) answers scripted JSON per call for every test, and a yard too old for a call is a standing case with standing words: `lines_too_old`, `grants_too_old` and their kin in `src/model.rs` turn "no such method" into a pane that says so, once, with the terminal command that does it there.

## 3. The proposal

The smallest version that answers the question: one new section in Decide listing the parked beads, one card per bead offering exactly the moves `yardr review` offers, and two new socket calls — a read that serves review's queue for one reader, and a write that makes one of its moves — with the Lines bead detail gaining the same card moves as a second step. The mechanisms that carry it already exist: review's queue and its move labels in the yard, `UserAsks`' per-reader queue shape for the read, the web bead page's revision-checked move for the write, and footplate's ask cards, `Bead` read and per-yard following for the drawing. What is new is only: the section and its row, the moves block on the card, the two calls, and the count that joins them. No pick of an ask moves a bead except as it does today (a stage ask's answer); a move here is a move, named as one, and nothing else in the window learns to move beads.

### 3.1. Which beads stand there

The rule is the parked half of `attention`, computed in the yard, not review's queue whole and not footplate's remarks. A bead stands in the section where all of these hold:

- it is open, and the reader may see it (the read lists beads for its viewer, as review's queue and `UserAsks` do; a bead of a linked yard, `bead@peer`, never stands here — it has no stage in this yard, and its asks already stand in Decisions from that yard's queue);
- its stage is worked by a manual group — the group that holds it, else the one its routes send it to, as both review and the web waits resolve it — and, where that group lists members, the reader is one of them (a group that lists none is anyone's, the operator included only as anyone is; an operator who is no member sees no row, exactly as in review, the yardmaster's wider role being yardr-837b's and left out below);
- it stands past the first stage of the flow that governs it — its type's flow where there is one, else its depot's, as Home's `firstStage` resolves it — so the backlog, where beads start, stands here never (open question 2 puts that to the decider; the yard's sidings stand past the first stage and do stand here, as they do in review and in `attention`, folded by nobody);
- the reader's part in it is unanswered: it is not the case that the reader is a member with another member left and has decided every ask to them on the bead (the web's answered rule, so Decide, Home and `/line` count the same beads; open question 4 puts keeping such a bead, with its "you answered" words, to the decider);
- and it carries no open ask to the reader. While one stands — a stage ask, which answering moves the bead, or any ask a person wrote — the bead stands in Decisions alone and never twice; when that ask is answered, declined, taken back or put aside (a put-aside ask stands among the decided, so the bead joins this list while the question sleeps, as it does on `/line`), the bead takes its place here on the `noted` event's re-read, if it still waits.

Faults stand here never: a held, prompt-blocked or stranded bead in a stage no person works needs fixing, mailing or releasing, not a move, and Lines already draws those beads with their remarks on the panel, the board and the bead's details (open question 3 puts full `attention` to the decider). A held bead stands nowhere here until it is released, even in a stage a person works: a hold is a fault, and Lines draws it as one.

The order is review's: longest waiting first — the bead's last change (`UpdatedAt`, the `since` the web's rows already carry as "about when it began to wait"), oldest first, the id breaking ties — so the queue is worked in the order it filled, as `UserAsks`' oldest-first queue above it is. The call names every bead; the list scrolls as the decisions list scrolls, with no cap and no folding: a member's parked beads number in tens at the busy end (section 3.6), and a folded count that hides the longest-waiting bead would spend the queue's one promise. Beads that are no work (a crew member's journal, a memory) never stand here: the queue reads work beads as review's does, where `UserAsks` must ask for the journals on purpose.

### 3.2. Where they stand, and what the row and card show

In Decide, under the open decisions and above the folded Decided, stands a section of its own headed "Waiting", with the waiting number in quiet words at its right as every head in the window carries its count (`waiting_words` in `src/window/words.rs`: "none waiting", "3 waiting"). Asks stand first because a question carries its context and its recommendation and asks for words; a parked bead asks only for a move, and stands next; what was dealt with stands under both, as it does today; the operator's Grants, Teammates and Pair rows keep the list's foot. The section stands only where the yard serves the read (section 3.4): on a yard too old for it there is no section and no words about it in Decide, because a permanent apology in the list would bury the asks it stands under. On such a yard the Lines bead detail alone carries one generic stage-help line where its moves would stand (the trigger is section 3.4's, and it is not the moves block: it names the stage's mechanism, not this reader, and offers no button). The rail's Decide tile, the switcher's rows and the dock's number count the waiting beads with the decisions (section 3.5), so a person in Crew or Lines sees the number before they switch.

The yardmaster's reasoning is taken for the primary surface, and mckean's Lines filter is refused as one: the complaint is keeping track of where to act, and a filter on Lines makes a second place to remember, over beads whose stage the person must then interpret. The bead detail in Lines gains the same moves anyway (bead 5 in section 6), because a person who finds the bead on the panel first should not be sent elsewhere to move it — but it is the same card block, over the same read, never a second queue with its own rule.

One bead's row, in the list's own shape (the question's place taken by the title, since a parked bead has no question):

```
┌ Decide ──────────────────┐  ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Decisions      2 waiting │  │ plantime                             │
│ Move the links? · yardm… │  │ Release the autumn timetable          │
│ Depot rules: 4 questions │  │ plantime-4f2a · ready · people       │
│                          │  │ waiting 3d · depot plantime          │
│ Waiting          3 waiting│  │                                      │

│  siding · people · 1d  9c1│  │ notes                                │
│  Take the new fares liv… │  │ assembly: folded 6 beads, tests pass │

└──────────────────────────┘  └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The row leads with the bead's title wrapped to two lines, then in the meta line its stage, its group and how long it waits, with the bead's short id small at the row's end — the decisions row's furniture, no new row kind. A depot's name joins the meta line where the yard has more than one depot with parked beads for the reader (in the mock it stands on the card; in a one-depot yard the row stays as drawn). Nothing on the row is a badge, and the waiting number in the section head is quiet words, never a tile: the yardr-design kit's counts rule, which the panel summary already keeps.

The card is the kit's one card (a Decision item: raised surface, subtle border, no coloured edge) in the pane where a decision card stands. Its head names the yard first, as every card's header does, then the bead's title, under it the bead whole in one line — id, stage, group, depot, how long it waits — and the bead's body and notes under it from the `Bead` read the window already makes for a card, with the route strip and the "Open" to the bead's web page where the view's address is set, as the Lines bead detail draws them (`talk_bead` in `src/window/bead.rs`). Under the notes stand the moves, one button each, labelled in the yard's words exactly as served — `done: move to build`, `send back to shape (note required)`, `Move to …` where the flow names no outcomes, `finish as merged (closes the bead)` where the move closes the bead, `Return to draft` for the stage whose question brought it — and one line for the move's note under them, with `i` typing in it and `esc` leaving it as the ask cards hold their keys. A move that needs a note (a `note_required` one: a move back to an earlier stage, the return to a question's asker excepted, whose answer is already on the bead) stands faint with "needs a note" until the line holds words; the others take the line's words where it holds any, and move without where it holds none. Picking a move with the number keys or the arrows names it on the button (`Send back to shape ↵`), and Enter makes the move the button names, as an ask card's Enter sends the pick its button names; a click makes it at once. What a person types and sees, for the send-back above:

```
note [ the printed fares fail the check on page 2        ]
     [ Send back to shape ↵ ]
→ the bead leaves Waiting; its row in Lines stands at shape,
  with the note on it for whoever picks it up there.
```

A `flow_error` card offers no button: the error stands where the moves would, in words, with the bead still readable whole — no move exists to offer until the flow is fixed, as review says. A `template_error` card shows the error where its ask would stand and offers its moves under it, as review does. While the yard is away the card's moves stand faint with the reason under them, as a conversation's Send does; nothing is queued, and the move is made again by hand when the yard is back.

### 3.3. What a move does, and what stands when it fails

A move is one write (section 3.4) carrying the bead, the outcome or the stage picked, the note's words where the line holds any, and the revision the row and card were read from. The yard checks, in its own write, that the bead still stands where the card read it, that the move is one offered to this reader (a manual stage of theirs, the flow allowing it), and that a note stands where the move needs one; the store's existing gates do all of that (`Expect`, `checkAdvance` and the stage-group check inside `moveBead`, yardr `internal/store/beads.go` and `internal/store/flows.go`), and the call adds the offered-move check the web's `beadMove` makes today, so a client can neither invent a move nor repeat one from a stale card. The note goes with the move in the same write, as review's does — never a note first and a move after, which a refused move would leave behind lying.

On the answer, the bead leaves the section at once and the next waiting row opens, as an answered ask leaves Decisions; the `advanced` event then re-reads the section, the arrivals and the flows together (section 3.5), so where the bead went stands in Lines without a second mechanism. A refused move writes nothing and says why whole on the card — the yard's words, as a run card shows a refusal — and a stale one ("bead … is at revision …, not …; read it again", the web's words) re-reads the bead and the section before it says so, so the card the person re-decides on is the bead as it stands. A move the call never answers (the yard went away mid-move) may have run, by the window's standing timeout rule: the card says "the yard did not answer; read the card again", never that the bead moved, and reads the section and the bead again; the move is not made again by the window. Making the same move twice by hand is refused by the revision check, not by remembering: the second card is stale on its face.

Nothing else moves: putting a parked bead aside, holding or releasing it, claiming it, and following its diff are not offered (section 4). There is no Later for a parked bead — leaving it is just leaving, and the oldest-first order keeps it where the queue put it.

### 3.4. The yard calls: one read, one write, both new

yardr needs two new stable calls. Both take their arguments by name, as every call of `Calls` does; both are served on the socket and, as `Calls`, to a device over the listener with the caller as its user; and where a device paired sealed, every call of the client is sealed alike, so neither needs a sealing rule of its own (a move carries the person's note words and nothing secret — no `GiveSecret` rule applies to it). "With the caller as its user" is a binding the listener makes, not a word the client supplies: yardr adds both calls to `selfArg` in `internal/rpc/user.go`, beside `UserAsks` and `AnswerAsk` there today, so that over the listener each identity argument below is filled with the device's user where it is left out and refused, with no call made, where it names anyone else. On the socket the identity arguments are the caller's word, as they are for `AnswerAsk` today: the socket's caller stands on the yard's machine, and the store's own gates (the offered-move rebuild and `moveBead`'s membership check below) still judge that word.

**`Waiting` (read)** — the parked queue for one reader. In `Calls` as `{"name", "actor", "viewer"}`, exactly `UserAsks`' arguments (footplate sends `name` alone, as it sends `UserAsks` today; the listener fills actor and viewer with the device's user under the `selfArg` binding above, `Waiting: {"name", "actor", "viewer"}`, and refuses a device that names another user, so no device reads another person's queue). Its result is one row per bead, longest waiting first:

| field | what it is |
|---|---|
| `bead`, `title` | the bead and its title |
| `depot` | the depot it is worked in, absent where it has none |
| `stage`, `group` | where it stands, and the manual group whose stage it is |
| `since` | when the bead last changed (RFC 3339), as `UserAsks`' `since` |
| `revision` | the bead's revision the row was read at, for the move |
| `actions` | the moves offered to this reader, in review's order: each with `kind` (`done` or `advance`), its `label` whole as review prints it, the `outcome` or the `to` stage it makes, `note_required` and `terminal` where they hold; empty where a `flow_error` stands |
| `flow_error` | why the bead's flow does not resolve, where it does not |
| `template_error` | why entering the stage put no stage ask, where the template broke |

The yard computes the rule of section 3.1 in the store, reusing what review and the web already compute: the routed manual group and its membership, the governing flow's first stage, the open asks to the reader from the same queue `UserAsks` reads, and the moves from `NextStages` read for the bead — its outcomes as they stand for the bead, else its allowed stages, with the return stage — labelled with review's words, round notes and all, so the terminal, the web and the window can never offer one bead two different moves. (Review's labelling lives in `internal/cli/review.go` and the web's copy in `internal/web/acts.go` today; the yardr bead in section 6 lifts one copy where all three read it, rather than adding a third.) Its socket class is `Read`, as `UserAsks`' is.

**`MoveBead` (write)** — one offered move. In `Calls` as `{"bead", "outcome", "to", "note", "revision", "actor"}`: exactly one of `outcome` (a `done` action) and `to` (an `advance` action) is given, as the web's move form gives them; `note` is left out where the line is empty; `actor` is who moves it. That last word is bound, not chosen, wherever a device calls: yardr adds `MoveBead: {"actor"}` to `selfArg` in `internal/rpc/user.go`, so the listener fills `actor` with the device's user where it is left out and refuses the call, making no call at all, where it names anyone else — a device therefore cannot name another member of the bead's stage group as `actor`, however the rest of the call reads. The yard then resolves the bead, refuses where the revision is not the bead's now, rebuilds the offered moves for that bound actor as `Waiting` serves them and refuses a move not among them (naming what is offered, in the store's words), refuses a missing note where the move requires one, and then makes the store's `Done` or `AdvanceNote` with `Expect{Stage: the bead's stage as read under the revision, Revision: revision}`; inside that write `moveBead`'s `ActMoveOut` check judges the same bound actor against the stage's group, so a bound non-member is refused there too and nothing is written. Its answer names the bead, the stage it entered, and whether it closed (`closed`), as review prints `bead → stage (closed)`; footplate reads the section again on the `advanced` event in any case. Its socket class is `Admin`, as `AnswerAsk`'s answering is: a person or the crew may make it from outside a pane, a bead's own agent in its pane may not move beads with it (its own `done` is the agent's call, unchanged). It is not in `READS` on footplate's side: a timeout on it may have moved the bead, and the window says so (section 3.3).

No other call changes. `Bead` gains nothing: the revision rides the `Waiting` row, the card reads the bead whole as it does today and is re-read on every event whose bead it is, and a move decided from a card whose bead changed since is refused by the revision and read again — the web page's settlement, kept. `docs/api.md` gains the two calls (its test holds the page to the server), `docs/yards.md` gains one read row and one write row (footplate's test holds those tables to the calls made), and the stand-in yard in `src/fake.rs` answers both, scripted, so every test runs with no yard.

A yard too old for either call answers "no such method", which footplate already turns into the too-old case (`lines_too_old` and its kin in `src/model.rs`): the model keeps a `waiting_too_old` flag for the yard, as it keeps `arrivals_too_old`, the Waiting section is not drawn, and the rail and switcher count decisions alone as today. The one legacy surface is deliberately not per-reader, because no call such a yard serves can gate it per reader: `Flows` serves a stage's `group` and its `human` flag and no group's members (`FlowStage` in `src/api.rs`), and `waiting_on_you` in `src/model.rs` knows a bead only where its remark names the reader alone or the reader holds an open ask on it, which is exactly the multi-member wait this design exists for. Serving the members instead — a new field on `Flows` or a call of its own, kept only so an old yard could word a hint — is not proposed: it would grow a standing call for a yard that gains the whole feature by upgrading. So the Lines bead detail carries, in place of the moves block and only where every one of these holds, one generic stage-help line: the yard is `waiting_too_old`; the bead stands in a stage of the served `Flows` (so a bead in Elsewhere, in No line, or in no served flow shows nothing); and that stage's `human` flag is set (so a bead in a stage no person works shows nothing). The line is shown to every reader alike and names the stage's mechanism, never the reader — "this stage is moved by its people: `yardr review` on the yard's machine offers them its moves" — so it promises nothing about who may move this bead, and `yardr review` itself offers its moves to members alone, as it does today. It stands once, where the moves would stand in the detail the person already opened, never as a notice that repeats and never in Decide. On a yard that serves `Waiting` the line never stands: a bead in no `Waiting` row of that reader's offers neither the moves block nor the line (bead 5 in section 6 holds both halves). The read failing any other way is a notice, as the arrivals' failure is, and the failed read is made again as reads are.

### 3.5. Across the yards, and the counts agreeing

Every yard footplate follows carries its own queue, as it carries its own asks: `session::load` reads `Waiting` with the state (a "no such method" failure reads as `waiting_too_old` for that yard — no section, and the generic Lines line of section 3.4 armed subject to its `Flows` trigger; any other failure reads as no section, with why for the window to say, as an unreadable flows view does — never a yard held away for its queue), the model holds it beside the asks, and the follower re-reads it on the events that can change it. Those are the ones that already re-read arrivals and flows — every event about a bead, which covers `advanced`, `created`, `closed` and `reopened`, and the group, route, flow and depot changes named with `Flows` — plus `noted`, which already re-reads `UserAsks` (a stage ask answered by another member moves the bead; a writer's answer lifts the de-duplication). The read joins `Read` and `Effect` as `Asks` does, stamped like the rest, so a stale answer arriving after a newer one is dropped unread. A yard that is not open keeps its waiting number in its aside model's `Counts`, so the switcher row and the dock's sum stay live while the person works in another yard; opening the yard opens its Decide on the section's rows as they stand.

`Counts` (`src/window/state.rs`) gains the waiting number beside `decisions`: the Decide tile's badge is the two together, with the tooltip counting each apart ("2 decisions · 3 waiting"), the switcher's words gain "3 waiting" after the decisions, and the dock's total — mail and decisions today — counts the waiting beads too, since a parked bead waits on the person exactly as an ask does. The section's head number, the tile, the switcher and the dock are therefore one number read four ways, from the one read. The Lines panel's summary and the departures board's banner ("N waiting on you", opening Decide) count the same union from this read on: beads with an open ask to the reader, together with this section's beads — never the remark's "names you alone" approximation, which undercounts every multi-member stage (section 2). The words and the click stand; only the count's source changes, and the first footplate bead in section 6 makes that change with the section, so the two surfaces never disagree in a shipped build.

Newly parked beads raise no system notification in this design: the settings' kinds (mail, decisions, notices) gain none, and a bead parks whenever work is sent on, which is the yard's ordinary motion, where an ask is a person being asked. The badge, the switcher and the dock carry the number, as they carry decisions'; a notification kind returns if the count alone proves easy to miss (section 4).

On a phone, nothing here is new to build: the web view that already acts on these beads keeps its rows and its bead-page moves (its asked rows already give the question its first line whole at phone width), and a person reaching a yard from a phone browser moves parked beads there today, on a yard too old for `Waiting` alike, since the web view is the yard's own. In this window, at its floor, the section is the decisions list's furniture at the list's width: the row's title wraps to two lines and its meta line shortens as the decisions rows' do (`ui::shorten`'s budgets), the card's move buttons wrap whole at the kit's target size and the note line takes the card's width, so no move's words are cut at any window the window allows; the legacy stage-help line of section 3.4 is one wrapped line of the same detail at the same width, with no button to size. A phone footplate of its own (the design system's phone sizes and 48dp targets) would read and write these same two calls; drawing it is no part of this design.

### 3.6. The cost when a person is a member of busy stages

The read costs one pass in the yard: the open beads listed once for the viewer, the routes, groups and settings read once, and — the one real cost — the flow resolution `NextStages` makes, which reads the depot's repository flows. Review pays that per bead at a terminal, once, for one person; a read made on events cannot. The yardr bead therefore resolves each depot-and-type's flow once per `Waiting` call and reads each candidate's outcomes, return stage and template error from that resolution (the per-bead stay lookup review already makes), so a call over two hundred parked beads of one depot costs one flow resolution, not two hundred. For scale: `UserAsks`, re-read on every `noted` event today, lists every bead and resolves every bead's notes; `Waiting` reads no bead's notes except to know its open asks to the reader, which the same queue machinery already walks, and its moves need no notes at all. footplate pays one call per followed yard per bead event, as it already pays `Flows` on the same events; an aside yard's queue is re-read only for its count, which is the switcher's standing bargain for asks today.

For the person, a busy stage is a long list with the longest wait at its top and no bead hidden: two hundred parked beads are two hundred rows, reached by the scroll every list here already has, each opened as one `Bead` read. That is the honest shape of review's queue too; what it must never be is two hundred cards, or a count that sums stages the reader cannot act on. The membership rule is the bound that matters: only stages listing the reader (or listing nobody) cost them rows, so a yard with busy stages worked by other people costs this reader nothing — and a person who is a member of every busy stage has, in fact, that much waiting on them, which the dock's number should say.

## 4. Left out

**Faults in Decide (held, blocked and stranded beads in stages no person works).** Full `attention` would bring them under the same head. They are left to Lines — the panel's held count, the board's remarks, the bead's details — because no move this section offers fixes one: the actions there are reading the bead, mailing the crew and releasing a hold, none of which is a flow move, and a fault row among the moves would teach that moving a bead past its blockage is the answer. Open question 3 puts it to the decider, since the complaint ("keep track of where to act") argues the other way.

**The backlog.** Where beads start, shaped by the yardmaster, in numbers an order past the parked beads'. `yardr review -g backlog` keeps it at the terminal; footplate shapes nothing and this design adds no shaping surface. Open question 2 puts a folded backlog count to the decider.

**A `mine` filter on Lines as the surface.** Refused in section 3.2 as the primary place; the Lines bead detail's moves (bead 5) are not a queue and keep Lines for seeing, as the panel design settled. A filter that lists only the reader's stages' beads, as a view of Lines, returns only if the Decide section proves to be the wrong home in use.

**The diff on the card.** Review shows `git diff --stat` and pages the full diff; `Bead` serves neither, and serving diffs is a promise of its own (which base, dir depots' outputs, size). The card shows the bead whole; the bead's web page, one tap on its Open, and `yardr review` keep the diff. For stages whose decision is the diff (a review bead parked for a person), that is a real lack, named here rather than papered over.

**Dismissing, holding and folding from the section.** No Later, no hold or release, no claim, no fold of siding beads into their carrier: the section moves beads and nothing else. `yardr bead hold`, `release` and the yardmaster's fold stay where they are; a dismiss returns if parked rows prove to need one, with the use that asks for it.

**Telling the person outside the window.** No new notification kind, and the yard's own phone telling (`notify=` through a notify source) is the yard's, unchanged: it already tells of the inbox, asks among it. Counts in the window are this design's telling.

**The yardmaster role (yardr-837b) and the flow warning (yardr-v0dv).** Left out on purpose by the bead: an operator who is no member moves nothing here, and a flow that routes a stage to nobody warns nowhere new. **Beads of linked yards** wait in their own yard's window, where their stage stands; only their asks cross, as they do today. **Building any of it** is the beads of section 6, not this document.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Where the section stands and what it is called.** Option A, as proposed: a "Waiting" section of its own in Decide, under the open decisions and above Decided — costs the asks the list's foot, and a second head to learn. Option B: the waiting beads above the asks — costs every secret, run and setup card its first place for rows that ask only for a move. Option C: one interleaved queue by longest wait, asks and parked beads together — costs the topic grouping the asks stand under, and mixes questions with moves in one row kind. I would take A: the asks are the work that needs words, and the section's own head is what the complaint asks for — one named place.
2. **Whether the backlog stands in footplate at all.** Option A, as proposed: never, past-the-first-stage only — costs a person who shapes from the backlog their terminal trip, kept at `yardr review -g backlog`. Option B: a folded "Backlog · N" row at the section's foot, opening the same rows folded — costs the section a second queue's rows at backlog numbers, and a shaping surface's first step (opening one offers moves; shaping is notes and labels, which the card does not do). Option C: backlog rows in the section like any stage — costs the queue its bound; a busy yard's backlog swamps the beads that wait on a decision. I would take A: the backlog waits on shaping, not on this reader's move, and `attention`'s first-stage line is the yard's standing answer.
3. **Whether faults join the section.** Option A, as proposed: no; held, blocked and stranded beads stay in Lines — costs the person two places after all, faults in Lines and moves in Decide, against the complaint's letter. Option B: full `attention` under the Waiting head, faults first with their words, offering the bead whole and no move — costs the section its one verb (every row but the faults moves; the fault rows act by opening Lines' detail, a third behaviour in one list), and duplicates Lines' held count in a second home. I would take A: what a fault needs is not a move, and Lines is where its context (the stage, the session, the screen) already stands.
4. **A bead the reader has answered, where another member must move it.** Option A, as proposed: it leaves the section (Home's answered rule) — costs the answerer sight of it; if the other member never moves it, it waits where only `/line`'s parked rows name whose move it is. Option B: it stays, its meta line reading "you answered; waits for \<others\>" as `/line` words it — costs every multi-member stage's answered beads a permanent row in each answerer's list, and a count that sums beads its reader cannot act on. I would take A: Decide, Home and the dock must count the same beads, and the answerer's part is done.
5. **Whether a newly parked bead tells the person.** Option A, as proposed: counts only (rail, switcher, dock) — costs missing a bead that parks while the window is behind another, until the dock is looked at. Option B: a system notification as for a decision, under the Decisions kind of the settings — costs a notification for the yard's ordinary motion (every send-on into a manual stage tells), where an ask is a person asked on purpose. I would take A: the dock's number is the standing tell, and the settings gain a kind when a missed parked bead is observed, not before.
