# A window kit for footplate in yardr-design, then the views cleaned up one after another

Design footplate-p4gg, proposed 2026-11-11. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

Design for bead footplate-p4gg (flow `design.design`, stage `draft`). A yard is one installation of yardr: its beads, its crew and its depots. A bead is one durable piece of work with a stage and a history. footplate is the yard's native window (Rust, gpui), the place a person drives their yard from without a terminal. yardr-design is the design language that stands beside it: tokens for colour, type, spacing and motion, a set of components, and one kit, `ui_kits/yardr-panel`. A kit in that repository is a click-through composition of the components that shows how a surface is put together. A view in footplate is one mode or pane the window draws: Decide, a bead's view, the line views, chat, settings and the first run that sets a yard up. An ask is one question as data on a bead, and Decide is the window's decisions mode that puts the open asks in front of the person. A shot is a picture of the shipped window in a named state, taken by `scripts/shot-linux.sh` on Linux or `scripts/shot-mac.sh` on macOS against a fake yard (`src/shot.rs`), in light and in dark. See `docs/glossary.md` in the yardr repository for all of yardr's words.

## 1. The question

mckean, 10 Nov 2026, decided the start by mail (mail 174172, then mail 174189: "we can do the kit as you suggested", after the earlier "clean things up and keep them easy to look at. one after another"), and confirmed it again when the bead was sent (ask 23576, answered 11 Nov 2026: yes, send it now). His mockup of Decide from a separate session (mail 174063) is the direction for the look, not a specification. The question this document answers is the one the bead puts: the design language exists and every builder is sent to it first, but it has no window in it, so each view has drawn its own parts and the views have grown uneven — what window parts should yardr-design gain as a window kit, taken from the shipped window and not from a brief, where do footplate's colours and small drawing helpers already drift from the tokens and which side should move, and in what order should the views then be cleaned up, one after another, each changing nothing the view says or does?

## 2. What exists today

**The language exists; the window does not.** yardr-design's `readme.md` is the brand: the railway vocabulary kept whole (yard, bead, crew, depot, never "tasks" or "dashboard"), exactly one rust accent with green only for the running dot and red only for errors, counts as quiet words at the right and never a badge, targets of at least 32 pixels, one popover shadow, no gradients or blur in the app, and a light theme that is opt-in while yardr.dev stays always dark. Its tokens stand in `tokens/colors.css` (the dusk and ink scales, the yard rust scale 300 to 700, brass for illustration only, the signal green and red, and the semantic surfaces, borders, texts and states for the dark default and the light theme), `tokens/typography.css` (IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono as substitutes, five roles from title to code), `tokens/spacing.css` (the 2 to 24 spacing scale, radii 4, 6, 8 and full, a 32-pixel control height, a 40-pixel row height, a 52-pixel setting row, a 340-pixel sidebar and the one popover shadow), `tokens/motion.css` (120 and 180 milliseconds on one easing curve, nothing under reduced motion) and `tokens/components.css` (the component classes). Its components are thirteen files exporting fourteen names: Button and IconButton; Select, Switch, TextInput and SettingRow; StatusDot and StatusRow; AppHeader with its Lockup and SectionHeader (`AppHeader.jsx` exports both names); CrewRow and DecisionItem; and Icon, the one wrapper for the Lucide font. Its one kit, `ui_kits/yardr-panel` (a 340-pixel panel with a header, a status row, decisions and crew, and a settings pane), states in its own readme that no product screenshots or source were supplied, so its layout is built from the written brief and not copied from a shipped screen. That panel is the whole of what the kit knows about footplate today. The window itself — its rail, its three lists, its cards, its timetable — stands in no kit, and the four designs that drew window parts before this one (footplate-xrgz, footplate-zj5f for the rail, footplate-qskt for the Lines panel and footplate-7k20 for the waiting moves) each named their parts inside their own document.

**footplate draws the window from two places.** `src/theme.rs` holds the window's colours as one `Palette` for a light desktop and one for a dark desktop (the grounds, the line, the texts, the accent and what stands on it, the warning red, the link blue and the peer violet, the running green, the selection tint, the code ground and the popover shadow), the yard shades a yard is coloured by (`yard_color` and `yard_ink`, the rust scale by a stable hash of the yard's name), and the crew dot's colours (`crew_dot`). `src/window/ui.rs` holds the small pieces every view draws with: the rail glyphs drawn stroke by stroke (the track, the signal lamp, the semaphore arm, the settings gear and the terminal), the top band that stands under the macOS title bar (`band`), the centred card that floats over the window (`card_layer`, used by the About card and the Feedback form), the quiet, small and send-held buttons, the keys hint, the segmented control (`segment` and `segmented`), the dropdown chevron, the Select's field, list and option rows, the drawn checkbox, and `shorten`, which ends a string with an ellipsis after a character budget because gpui 0.2.2's `truncate()` clips mid-glyph in a shrinking flex item. `src/window/metrics.rs` holds the measures (the 64-pixel rail, the 624-pixel window floor of 560 plus the rail, the list at 270 pixels at the usual text size and never over 48 hundredths of the window, the reading width of 880 pixels, and the character budgets the rows are shortened by). `src/window/words.rs` holds what the heads and counts say and the colours that say what waits and for whom. A builder who changes anything that shows is sent to yardr-design first, by `AGENTS.md` and by the `eyes` rule in `.yardr/depot.md`, and then draws from these two files. The gap is therefore not a language but a kit: the tokens and the thirteen component files describe a 340-pixel panel, and the window is a 624-pixel floor with a rail, a list and a reading pane that the panel never had.

**The window as shipped, view by view.** The shell stands in `src/window/chrome.rs`. `Footplate::render` in `src/window.rs` picks what shows (the list with the open conversation, the settings sheet, the first run, the away screen) and stacks the switcher, the open picture, a session's screen, Feedback, About and the keys cheatsheet over it. The rail (`rail` in `src/window/chrome.rs`) stands on the far left at every size: the open yard's chip of its first two letters on its yard colour, the three mode tiles with a drawn glyph, a label under it and a count badge at the tile's corner, and at its foot the Feedback tile where it is offered and the Settings gear. The list column (`column`, `list` and `list_body`) stands beside it headed by the yard's status row, and its body is one of three lists by mode. Chat's list (`list_chat`) is the crew, the linked yards' people and this yard's people under their group heads, then the yard row under its own. Decide's list (`list_decisions` in `src/window/asks.rs`) is the open decisions grouped by topic, the Waiting section of parked beads, the folded Decided history a page at a time, and at its foot, where the window is the operator's, the Grants, Teammates and Pair-a-phone rows. The timetable's list (`list_timetable` in `src/window/arrivals.rs`) is the arrivals row, the lines in the served order, the No line row while beads travel with no line, and the quiet lines folded under one head. The reading pane (`talk` in `src/window/chrome.rs`) is the conversation, the yard's notices, a decision card, a waiting card, a decided card, the grants, teammates or pairing panes, the arrivals panel, one line's flows, the No line list, or one bead's details, by what is open.

**Decide is a list of rows and one card with many blocks.** A decision row (`decision_row`) leads with the bead's head wrapped to two lines, then each open ask's head and its metadata line, then the row's sub line, with the bead's short id at the row's end. A waiting row (`waiting_row` in `src/window/waiting.rs`) leads with the bead's title and names its stage, its group and how long it waits in its metadata line. The card (`decision_card` in `src/window/asks.rs`, with its words in `src/window/ask.rs`) stands in the reading pane and holds, in order: the yard's weight lines where the ask is weighty, the question with how long it waits beside it, who asked, the context lines, the disclosure that opens the bead whole (`bead details (e)` with the bead's id and the ask's number beside it, `ask_line`), the body of the ask by its kind (a secret ask's line and where its value goes, a run ask's block, a setup ask's block with its account candidates, or the numbered options with the recommended one named under its own row), the line for words or the secret and account fields with their Show control, any files attached to the answer with why a marked one cannot go, a refusal where the yard did not take the answer, the action row (the button that answers exactly as Enter does, Ask about this, Later and Decline, with the reason where the yard is reconnecting), and the keys line with the cheatsheet hint. A run block (`run_block`) and a setup block (`setup_block`) share their shape: a line that says in which yard and as whom approving runs, the command whole as one fenced block that copies exactly (`fenced` in `src/window/ask.rs`), what the command does, the yard's own sentences for what it changes, and the end of the block (`block_end`) with the ask's own texts behind one disclosure and the commands that take the change back. A teammate ask's head (`teammate_head`) stands in the same block where the command proposes one. The waiting card (`talk_waiting` and `moves_block` in `src/window/waiting.rs`) offers the moves the yard serves a parked bead, one button each, with one line for their note, and stands in the bead's details in Lines as well.

**A bead's view, the lines and chat each draw their own heads and rows from the same helpers.** A bead's details (`talk_bead` in `src/window/bead.rs`) head with a quiet back link that names where the bead was opened from, the bead's title, its id with its line and stage, who works it with the control that opens that session's screen, the strip of its route (`route_strip` in `src/window/chrome.rs`, one dot and name per stage), when it arrives with its remark in its tone's colour, and its labels, and then hold the bead's body and its notes with each note's author and time and its pictures and files (`bead_body`, shared with the card's expanded bead as `expanded_body`). The timetable's arrivals pane (`talk_arrivals`, `panel`, `panel_track`, `panel_cell` and `panel_headcode` in `src/window/arrivals.rs`, with `src/window/panel.rs` for its heads and tracks) draws each flow as one straight track of equal stage cells with headcodes for its beads, a departures board (`departures_board`) of strict columns (the expected arrival in the mono face, the bead, the platform, the line and the remark), and one line's stages as columns of bead cards (`stage_column` and `bead_card`), with the backlog as a count and its first titles. Chat's conversation (`talk_conversation`, `mail_rows`, `entry`, `entry_head` and `entry_body` in `src/window/bead.rs`) is the reading column of letters with a day break between days (`day_break` and `reading_column` in `src/window/panel.rs`), each letter's head with its sender and a clock that stands in its gutter under the pointer, its body drawn from the light Markdown in `src/markdown.rs` with its pictures, pages and files, the bar of reactions that stands over a letter under the pointer, the preview of an ask that waits inside a conversation, and the composer at its foot with the one button that is voice, send or stop by what the box holds and whether a voice session stands. The yards' views (`src/window/yards.rs`) are the switcher that stands over the window to the rail's right, each yard's card with its login switch and agents settings, the pairing and signing-in forms, and the away screen with its reconnecting strip. The settings sheet (`talk_settings`, `view_setting`, `run_setting`, `voice_setting` and `telemetry_setting` in `src/window/chrome.rs`) is one sheet of named settings, each with its keys or its help line under its name and its control under that, standing over whatever was open. The first run (`src/window/firstrun.rs`, its commands in `src/setup.rs`) is its own stepped surface for setting a yard up here. Every one of these surfaces can be pictured without a real yard: `src/shot.rs` names 86 states (among them `decisions`, `decisions-filtered`, `decisions-more`, `plain`, `run`, `secret`, `weighty`, `waiting`, `lines-waiting`, `arrivals`, `departures`, `arrival`, `flow`, `crew`, `thread`, `column`, `voice`, `settings` and its sections, `setup`, `switcher`, `away` and `grants`), and `scripts/shot-linux.sh` and `scripts/shot-mac.sh` take each state in light and in dark.

**Where the colours and the helpers already drift from the tokens.** The check the bead left undone is made here against `src/theme.rs` and `tokens/colors.css`, with the contrast read as the tests in `src/theme.rs` read it. The rust agrees: the accent is the yard 500 (`#D3602F`) in both looks on both sides, the yard shades are the rust scale whole (300, 400 and 500 on dark grounds; 500, 600 and 700 on light), the text accent's intent is a rust step that reads as text, and the popover shadow's colour and fall (six pixels down, twenty of blur, the dark ground at four tenths and the light at twelve hundredths) are the token's. Everything else has moved apart, in both directions. On dark grounds footplate's grounds (`#1E2024`, `#17181B`, the open row at `#2C3038` and the hover at `#23262C`) stand a step lighter and bluer than the token's dusk surfaces (`#161718` for the app, `#1C1D1F` raised, `#232426` hover, `#2A2B2E` pressed), and its line (`#33363C`) is the token's strong border (`#333538`) with more blue in it; on light grounds footplate's grounds (`#FBFAF8`, `#F0EEEA`, the open row at `#E0DCD4`) stand warmer and browner than the token's neutral paper (`#F7F7F6`, `#EDEDEB`, `#E2E2DF`), where the token's mood is a neutral graphite with no blue cast and no warm one. The secondary text has drifted with the grounds: footplate's faint (`#8D939C` on dark, `#6E727A` on light) stands between the token's two ink steps and darker than the token's secondary (`#ABADB0` on dark at eight to one, `#5B5E64` on light), reading at about five to one on footplate's own grounds where the token's secondary reads at six to eight. The text accent has drifted a step each way for the same reason (footplate takes rust 300 on dark and rust 700 on light where the token takes 400 and 600). On dark the token's step would read at 4.45 to one on footplate's present open row, under the four-and-a-half the window holds its texts to (`src/theme.rs`'s text-accent test, on side, ground, hover and open), where footplate's lighter step reads past six; on the token's own pressed ground the same step reads at 4.76, so the dark drift is a consequence of the grounds and not of the step. On light the token's step fails on both sides' grounds: rust 600 reads at 3.77 on footplate's present open row and at 3.97 on the token's own pressed ground, and at 4.39 on the token's hover ground, so no change of grounds makes it pass, where footplate's rust 700 reads at 5.72 on the token's pressed ground and past six on its hover. What stands on the accent has split: the token fills its primary button one step deeper (rust 600, `#B54E22`) so that white stands on it at five to one and warns that white on rust 500 reads at only 3.8, while footplate fills every accent control (the primary button, the chosen segment and the checked box) with rust 500 itself and stands near-black (`#161718`) on it, which reads at 4.7 and is pinned by its test — two honest answers to the same contrast, with different fills. The warning red (footplate `#F0764F` on dark and `#B3401C` on light against the token's signal red `#E5675A` and `#B3382C`) and the running green (footplate `#7FB069` and `#3D8B4F` against the token's signal green `#4CC38A` and `#1F9A60`, the brighter of the two reading at eight to one where footplate's reads at six and a half) have each settled a shade apart, footplate's red nearer the rust and its green quieter than the token's. Three colours footplate draws with have no token at all: the link blue (`#7AA2F7` and `#1A56C8`, held by its own test with the note that no design token names a blue), the linked name's peer violet (`#B693F5` and `#6440B8`), and the translucent ground under code and the tint of a selection, for which the token offers only the sunken surface under a decision's single-line detail. The helpers drift in kind as well as in colour. The token's Switch is a 36-by-20 track with a sliding thumb for an on-or-off setting and its Button is a filled or bordered 32-pixel control; the window has no switch and no filled button kind of its own, and says on-or-off with a segmented control (`segment`, its chosen segment filled with the accent) and yes-or-no kinds with a drawn checkbox of 32 pixels, answers a card with a bordered small button, and ends a status row with a quiet button that is words alone. The token's TextInput stands on the sunken ground with a strong border; the window's one field kind (the card's words, secret, account and type-back fields, and the composer's line) stands on the side ground with the hairline border at three by two of padding. The token's Select is a native select with a chevron over it; the window's Select is drawn whole (a 32-pixel field, a list that stands inline under it and scrolls, and its option rows), because gpui has no native select to stand behind it. The window sizes its text as one scalable number (the settings' text size as a rem of 16 pixels at the usual size, the text itself at seven eighths of it, `Settings::rem` in `src/settings.rs` and `ROOT_TEXT_REM` in `src/select.rs`) where the token's roles are fixed pixel sizes, and it draws its rail glyphs, its chevron and its check mark stroke by stroke where the token gathers its glyphs in one Lucide font, with the one settled exception that a first-level rail of mode icons with a label under each glyph stands outside the header.

**The Decide mockup beside the shipped Decide.** The shipped Decide has no card in the mockup's sense. `talk_decision` in `src/window/asks.rs` builds a padded, scrolling list, and each `decision_card` in it is a plain column of lines with no background, no border and no padding of its own; only the blocks inside it are cards, the run block and the setup block standing bordered on the side ground. The mockup (mail 174063) instead puts one raised, bordered card in front of the person, with a summary box and its own sections inside it. The two agree on direction — the counts as quiet words, the one rust spent on what waits on the person reading, the question leading — and differ in structure, so the kit must say which part comes from where: the Ask card's contents and slots (section 3.1) are recorded from the shipped window, and its one raised, bordered outer frame is taken from the mockup, as the direction mckean set, and is first drawn by the Decide cleanup rather than copied from any shot. What the mockup's card holds beyond the frame — its count of changes and its two sections for what changes and what already matches — is a second difference, and not for want of data from the yard. For a depot pack the yard already sends the effect as data: `run.pack.plan.objects` carries each object's kind, name and action with its changes (the yard's `AskPlanObject`), and the card's opening sentence already counts the actions. footplate receives those fields and drops most of them: `RunPlanObject` in `src/api.rs` keeps only an object's name, its hashes, its new text and its diff, and the run block draws the yard's sentences as prose with the texts behind one tap, so no count and no grouping by action is drawn. That content is footplate work that remains (section 4), not a call the yard lacks.

## 3. The proposal

The smallest version that answers the question: one new kit in yardr-design, `ui_kits/footplate-window`, composed from the components that already stand and a small set of window parts named from the shipped window's shots, with the one frame taken from the mockup as section 2 says; a token-by-token settlement of where `src/theme.rs` and `src/window/ui.rs` move and where the tokens move instead; and then six cleanup beads in footplate, one view each in the bead's order, each standing on the kit and changing nothing its view says or does, landing one after another because the window's drawing files are shared. No new behaviour, no new setting, and no new word in any view is proposed anywhere in it. What a person would type and see changes nowhere: the kit is drawn in the design repository as HTML and the cleanups redraw the same words, rows and cards in the kit's measures, so the proposal shows its surfaces as the shots name them rather than as new screens.

### 3.1. The window kit: few kinds, each from a shot but one frame

The kit lives beside the panel as `ui_kits/footplate-window` in yardr-design, with its own readme that says, as the panel's does, what it is composed from — here, that it is copied from the shipped window's shots and not built from a brief, naming the states each frame was taken from, and naming the Ask card's outer frame as the one part taken from mckean's mockup instead of from a shot. It composes the components that already stand (Button, IconButton, Select, Switch, TextInput, SettingRow, StatusDot, StatusRow, AppHeader and SectionHeader, CrewRow and DecisionItem where the panel's sense of them is the window's too) and adds the window parts below. The parts are named for what is already drawn, in the drawing code's own words where it has good ones, and each is evidenced by named shot states in light and in dark on Linux and on macOS, so a reader can put the kit's frame and the shipped window side by side. Seven kinds are new; everything else the window draws is one of the components that already stand, one of these seven, or a view's own arrangement of them that no second view shares and that therefore stays out of the kit.

The seven, in the order a window is read from its edge to its centre:

1. **Rail tile and yard chip.** The window's shell: a 64-pixel rail (a tile of 52 pixels with a drawn glyph of 28, a 10-pixel label under it and, where its mode holds what waits, a count badge at its top right in the accent where the wait is the person's and muted where it is not), headed by the yard chip (40 pixels, the yard's first two letters on its yard colour) and footed by the settings tile. It stands from `rail` in `src/window/chrome.rs` and the glyphs in `src/window/ui.rs`, evidenced by every state, since the rail stands in all of them. The kit's AppHeader is the panel's 44-pixel header and does not cover it; the rail is the window's header turned on its side, and the design system's one settled exception for it (railway glyphs with a label, outside the header) is recorded with the kind. Its measures are fixed pixels, as the rail's are, because it carries no reading text.
2. **List row.** One row for every list the window keeps: a head that wraps to two lines, one metadata line under it for each further fact, and a short id or a count in quiet words at the row's end, with the open row filled, the row under the pointer told by its hairline alone, and a state dot and a peer suffix where the row carries them. It stands from the one shell that `row` in `src/window/chrome.rs`, `decision_row`, `grants_row`, `teammates_row` and `pairing_row` in `src/window/asks.rs`, `waiting_row` in `src/window/waiting.rs`, `arrival_row` in `src/window/arrivals.rs` and the decided rows already repeat between them (one margin, padding, radius and border that appears only under the pointer), evidenced by `crew`, `decisions`, `waiting`, `arrivals` and `lines`. The kit's CrewRow is this row's panel form and DecisionItem its card form; the window kit keeps the one List row and names CrewRow's title, sub and state as its head, metadata and end, so the panel and the window stop describing the same row twice.
3. **Section head.** A section's title on the left and its count in quiet words on the right, with its folding form (the same head with the disclosure mark before its title, a hover and a pointer, for the Decided and Waiting folds and the timetable's Quiet) and its plain form with no count for a chat group's head. It stands from `section_head`, `fold_head` and `group_head` in `src/window/ask.rs` and the panel's heads in `src/window/panel.rs`, evidenced by `decisions`, `decisions-filtered`, `waiting-folded` and `crew`. The kit's SectionHeader already draws the plain form at the panel's measures; the window kind keeps its name and adds the fold, rather than growing a second head.
4. **Detail head.** The head of a reading pane: a quiet back link that names where the view was opened from at its top, then the title, then one metadata line of the ids and places that name the thing whole, then the further lines the thing carries (who works it, when it arrives with its remark in its tone, its labels), with the route strip as its one sub-part (one dot and name per stage of its flow, the bead's own stage in the text colour and semibold, the stages behind it faint and filled, the ones ahead faint and open). It stands from the heads of `talk_bead` in `src/window/bead.rs`, `talk_decision` and `talk_decided` in `src/window/asks.rs` and the arrivals and flow heads in `src/window/arrivals.rs`, evidenced by `arrival`, `plain`, `run` and `departures`. No component covers it: AppHeader is one line of 44 pixels that never wraps, and the detail head is the pane's whole first band.
5. **Ask card.** The one card kind, at the reading pane's measures, in the raised, bordered frame the mockup sets and no shipped shot shows (section 2): the weight lines in the text rust where the ask is weighty, the question as its title with its age beside it, who asked, its context, its disclosure, its kind's body, its field, its files and refusal, its action row and its keys line. Its named slots, drawn once inside it and nowhere claimed as kinds of their own, are the option row (a numbered pick with its consequence, the chosen one filled), the disclosure (the bead whole behind one tap, with the bead's id and the ask's number standing beside it to select and copy), the command block (a line that says where and as whom the command runs, the command whole fenced with its copy control, what it does, and the yard's sentences), the code chip (a reference or a run of code in the mono face on the code ground, a link's chip in the link blue), and the callout line (a weight line in the text rust, a refusal or a notice in the warning red with its mark, each one line of plain words). It stands from `decision_card`, `run_block`, `setup_block` and `block_end` in `src/window/asks.rs` with `src/window/ask.rs` for its words, and from `talk_waiting` and `moves_block` in `src/window/waiting.rs` for the waiting card's form of it, evidenced by `plain`, `plain-select`, `run`, `secret`, `secret-shown`, `weighty`, `teammates`, `waiting` and `lines-waiting`. The kit's DecisionItem is this card's panel form (who, when, the ask, one detail line and Allow and Deny); the window kind is the card whole, and the panel's form is kept as the panel's. The guess's change row (a row with a kind, a name and a chip for each file a command would change) is refused as a kind: the shipped window draws no such row — its run card holds the command, the yard's sentences (which already count a depot pack's actions in prose) and its texts whole — and a row that counts and sorts the plan's objects by action is footplate rendering that has not been built, on data the yard already sends (section 4), so there is no shipped drawing for a kit to record.
6. **Timetable parts.** The lines' own drawings, sharing the headcode and the lamp between them: the panel track (one straight track per flow of equal stage cells on one grid, a cell lit only while a bead stands on it, its headcode plain, weighted, rust or red by the bead's own state with the running dot beside a worked one, and its count of the rest in quiet words), the departures row (the expected arrival first in the mono face, then the bead with its headcode, the platform as a plain word, the line and the remark in its tone), and the stage column (a column per stage with its group words under its name and one bead card under it, the kit's one card at a column's width). It stands from `panel`, `panel_track`, `panel_cell` and `panel_headcode`, `departures_board`, `talk_flow`, `stage_column` and `bead_card` in `src/window/arrivals.rs` with `src/window/panel.rs` and `src/window/words.rs` for their heads, lamps and words, evidenced by `arrivals`, `departures`, `flow`, `arrival` and `lines`. These are one family because the cell, the board row and the column card are three zooms of the same served bead, and the marker set that colours them is already one rule.
7. **Composer and field.** The one field kind and what stands around it at a pane's foot: a bordered field on the side ground that takes a line of words, a secret or an account (with its Show control beside it where it hides what is typed), the attachments that stand over the line, the reason that stands beside a held send, and the composer's one button that is voice, send or stop with the voice session's caption and meter beside it where one stands. It stands from the card's fields in `decision_card` and `typeback_lines`, from `talk_conversation` and its composer control in `src/window/bead.rs`, and from `select_field`, `select_list` and `select_option` in `src/window/ui.rs` where a setting picks from a list, evidenced by `plain`, `secret`, `voice`, `voice-ready`, `column` and `reconnecting`. The token's TextInput covers its panel form; the window kind records the window's ground, border and padding as its own, for the reason section 3.2 gives.

Two drawings that recur are deliberately not kinds. The centred card that floats over the window (`card_layer`, the About card and the Feedback form) is the kit's popover shadow on a raised card and is recorded as the window's use of the shadow, not as a new kind, since nothing else floats. The settings setting (a name, its keys or help in quiet words under it, and its control) is the kit's SettingRow at the sheet's measures with the window's segmented control, checkbox and Select standing where the panel's Switch and Select stand, and is likewise recorded as a use, with the control settlement of section 3.2, rather than as an eighth kind. Few kinds is the design system's own first principle, and both drawings are one component's use in a second place.

### 3.2. Where each side moves

The settlement is made per drift, and the mover is named for each. The rule that decides it is the one the contrasts already follow: a colour that only reads because of the ground it stands on moves with its ground, and a colour the token defines for every surface moves the ground under it. The kit bead (bead 1 in section 6) makes every token-side move below and no footplate move; the footplate moves land with the cleanup beads as section 3.3 orders them, the shared palette move once with the first of them.

The grounds move on footplate's side. `Palette`'s grounds, line, open and hover take the token's dusk and paper hexes whole in both looks (the app, sunken, raised, hover and pressed surfaces and the subtle and strong borders), because the token's neutral graphite is the brand's mood and footplate's bluer darks and warmer lights are the drift, not a choice any view depends on: no words, measures or tests name the present hexes except the palette's own contrast tests, which are re-proved on the new grounds in the same bead. The secondary text moves with them, taking the token's secondary on each ground, since its present step was chosen against the present grounds and the token's step reads further on the token's. The text accent moves on dark and stands on light, and the light step moves on the tokens' side instead. On dark footplate takes the token's step (rust 400) once the grounds have moved: its present lighter step exists only to pass on the present open ground, and on the token's grounds the token's step passes on every surface the window's test holds it to, at 4.76 to one on the pressed ground at worst. On light footplate keeps rust 700 and the token's light `--text-accent` moves from rust 600 to rust 700 with the kit, because the token's present step fails the same four-and-a-half on the token's own hover and pressed grounds (4.39 and 3.97 to one, as section 2 reads them) and the deeper step passes there at 5.72 at worst; the window's test is then re-proved unchanged, on the new grounds and the settled steps in both looks. The warning red and the running green move on footplate's side to the token's signal red and signal green in both looks, because both are signals the token defines once for every surface, footplate's quieter green and rust-nearer red name nothing the token's do not, and the crew dot and the status dot are the same dot in both kits and should be the same green. What stands on the accent does not move on either side; instead the split the token already holds is named in both places. The token keeps its primary button (rust 600 with white) and its accent (rust 500) as two fills, footplate splits `Palette` the same way (a button fill of rust 600 with white on it for the filled button, and the accent at rust 500 with near-black on it kept for the chosen segment, the checked box and the rail's waiting badge, where a segment's or a box's fill is a state and not a button), and each kit records that a button and a state never share a fill. This is the one place the proposal grows a palette field, and it grows it by taking the token's existing field rather than by inventing one.

Three colours move on the tokens' side, by gaining what the window already proves. The link blue becomes a token (one blue, for links that open an address and for a reference's chip, at text contrast on every surface in either look, footplate's present two hexes taken as its first values), because a colour three views draw with (messages, ask and bead ids, and the small controls that open or reset an address) is a language colour and not one view's choice, and the token's silence about blue is the panel's gap rather than the window's invention; the peer violet gains a token beside it on the same terms, as the second half of a linked name. The code ground and the selection tint become tokens (a translucent ground that lifts code off whatever it stands on, and the accent at a quarter to a third for a selection), because the window draws code in messages, cards and the session's screen and selects text in all of them, and the panel's sunken surface under one detail line is not a ground a message can stand on. Brass stays illustration only and gains nothing.

The helpers settle by kind, not one by one. Buttons move on footplate's side to the kit's three (primary, secondary and quiet at 32 pixels with the token's hover and pressed steps), with the card's bordered small button read as the secondary at its smaller padding and the status row's words as the quiet, since the window's three button drawings already are those three kinds at unrecorded measures. On-or-off moves on the tokens' side: the window kit records the segmented control as the window's on-or-off and two-or-three-way control (its chosen segment filled, as `segment` draws it) and the drawn checkbox as its yes-or-no kind of 32 pixels, and the panel's Switch stays the panel's, because the window has lived with segments and boxes at every text size and a switch thumb drawn in gpui buys nothing the segment does not already say; the kit stops presenting the Switch as the one honest on-or-off control and presents the two as the panel's and the window's forms of one decision. The field does not move: the window kit records the window's field (the side ground, the hairline border, three by two of padding) beside the token's sunken TextInput as the window's form, since the field stands on the list's and the card's ground and not on a sunken one, and its ground is the side token the palette already names. The Select does not move either: the window kit records the drawn field, list and option rows as the window's Select, since there is no native select in gpui for the token's form to be, and the chevron that says the field opens a list is kept in both forms. Type does not move in size: the window kit records the window's roles at the token's weights (title, section, label, body, meta and code, semibold where the token is semibold and medium where it is medium) scaled by the window's one rem, since a window whose text size is a setting cannot take fixed pixel roles, and the token gains the one line that its roles are fixed where the surface has no text setting and scaled where it has one. The glyphs do not move: the window kit lists the window's drawn glyphs (track, lamp, semaphore, gear, terminal, chevron and check mark) with their canvases, and the Lucide font stays the panel's and the site's, the rail's settled exception standing as it is. Motion does not move: the window kit records that the window's hover and pressed steps are immediate in gpui where the panel fades them in 120 and 180 milliseconds, as an accepted difference of the native window and not a drift to chase.

### 3.3. The cleanups, one view after another

Each cleanup bead is an `eyes` bead. It takes the before shots of its view first (the named states, Linux and macOS, light and dark, at the window's floor and at 1200 by 800), redraws that view in the kit's kinds, measures and settled colours, changes nothing the view says or does (the same words, the same counts in the same words, the same keys, the same calls and the same order of rows, cards and stages), and takes the same shots after, with both sets on its note. It runs `.yardr/test` and leaves the test line the gate asks for. The beads land one after another, never two at once, because `src/window.rs`, `src/window/chrome.rs` and `src/window/ui.rs` stand under every view and two redraws in flight would spend their review on each other's moves. The palette and helper settlement of section 3.2 lands once, with the first cleanup bead, as that bead's first commit: `Palette` takes the token's grounds and signals and splits its button fill, the contrast tests are re-proved on the new grounds, and the three button kinds and the recorded field and Select forms are settled in `src/window/ui.rs`, so every later bead starts from the kit's colours and helpers and redraws only its own view's arrangement. The order is the bead's: Decide, a bead's view, the line views, chat, settings, and setup last as its own bead, because the first run's stepped surface shares nothing with the settings sheet but the helpers and reads clearest once they have settled.

## 4. Left out

**The mockup's card content.** The count of changes and the two sections for what changes and what already matches are still left out, as the bead that set this design settled, but the reason is footplate's and not the yard's. For a depot pack the yard already sends the effect as structured data (`run.pack.plan.objects` with each object's kind, name, action and changes, and the opening sentence's count of the actions), and footplate already receives it; what remains is footplate work of its own: `RunPlanObject` in `src/api.rs` must keep the kind, the action and the changes it now drops, the model must count the objects by action and split the changed from the unchanged, and the run block must draw that count and those two sections in place of, or beside, the yard's sentences it now draws as prose. No yard call, field or protocol change is needed, and no yardr bead is proposed or filed for it; it is a footplate bead of its own when mckean wants the content, and this document does not file that either, since the bead left the content out on purpose. The kit grows no change row for it in the meantime (section 3.1): when that work is done, the ask card gains a section and the card kind does not change.

**New behaviour of any view.** No view gains or loses a row, a card, a key, a call or a word in any cleanup. A view whose kit drawing would read better with different words keeps its words, and the want is filed where it is found rather than taken in a cleanup that promised to change nothing said.

**A Rust kit.** The window kit is drawn in yardr-design as HTML, as the panel is, and footplate goes on drawing in gpui from the kit's tokens, measures and kinds. No component is ported to Rust, no drawing code is generated from the kit, and no crate is shared between the design repository and the window. The kit is the record the builders read, as the tokens already are.

**The phone and signalbox.** A phone footplate stays parked where the rail design left it, and signalbox goes on reading the web view's JSON. The window kit takes its frames at the window's floor and at 1200 by 800 and says nothing about a phone's 48-unit targets beyond keeping the token's phone roles where they stand.

**The sessions' screen, the picture views and the cheatsheet as views.** The screen that stands over the window for one session, the full-size picture with its menu, and the keys cheatsheet are layers the window stacks over its views, not views with lists and panes of their own. They take the settled colours and the popover shadow with the palette move, and gain no cleanup bead; a want found in one of them is filed where it is found.

**Renaming anything.** The modes keep their labels (Crew, Decide and Lines on the rail), the views keep their names, and the kit's new kinds take the drawing code's words where they are good. No railway word is translated and no plain word is put first anywhere in the window, which is yardr.dev's rule and not the app's.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Whether the rail is a kit kind or the window's shell alone.** Option A, as proposed: the rail tile and yard chip stand as the window kit's first kind — costs the kit a shell the panel never had, and a frame that stands in every shot whether its view is the bead's or not. Option B: the kit begins at the list, and the rail stays footplate's own drawing recorded in no kit — costs every cleanup bead the rail's measures (its tile, its badge and its chip) to invent again or to copy from the code, which is how the views grew uneven. I would take A: the rail is the one part every view shares, and a kit that starts past it leaves the shared part unrecorded.
2. **Whether the ask card's slots are kinds of their own.** Option A, as proposed: the option row, the disclosure, the command block, the code chip and the callout line are named slots of the one Ask card and no more — costs a second view that wants a command block without a card (the setup ask's block already stands inside its card, and the take-back commands stand inside the block's end) to take the slot's drawing from the card's frame rather than from a kind of its own. Option B: the command block and the callout line stand as kinds beside the card — costs the kit two more kinds for drawings no surface outside a card uses today, against few kinds. I would take A: a slot earns its own kind when a second surface draws it outside a card, and none does yet.
3. **Whether settings and the first run are one cleanup or two.** Option A, as proposed: two beads, settings then setup, in that order — costs one more landing in a queue that already runs one after another, and the first run waits behind the settings sheet. Option B: one bead for both — costs that bead two surfaces with different files (the settings sheet in `src/window/chrome.rs` and the yards' cards in `src/window/yards.rs` against the stepped first run in `src/window/firstrun.rs`) and two sets of before and after shots, reviewed as one change. I would take A: each bead stays small enough to finish without asking, and the first run's steps are the surface a new yard is judged by and want a review of their own.
4. **Whether the switch settlement stands.** Option A, as proposed: the tokens record the segmented control and the checkbox as the window's on-or-off and yes-or-no forms, and the Switch stays the panel's — costs the design system two forms of one decision to keep honest, and a reader who meets both must learn that they are one decision in two surfaces. Option B: the window's settings cleanup draws a Switch in gpui and the settings sheet takes it where it now takes segments — costs that bead a new drawn control with its thumb's motion settled in a toolkit with no switch to copy, for settings whose present segments already read at every text size. I would take A: the honest-control rule is that a control look like what it is, and both forms do; the gain of one form everywhere is not worth a new control in the last bead of the queue.
5. **Whether the palette move lands with Decide or stands alone before it.** Option A, as proposed: the settlement of section 3.2 lands as the Decide bead's first commit, before its own redraw — costs that bead two changes in one landing (every view's grounds change with its palette, then Decide's arrangement), and its before shots are therefore taken before the palette commit and its after shots after both, so the pair shows the palette and the view together. Option B: a small palette bead lands first with shots of every view and no arrangement change — costs the queue one more landing whose whole change is colours, reviewed apart from any view it recolours, and every later bead's before shots move by that one step. I would take A: the queue stays at the bead's six, the palette's contrast tests travel with the card that proves them, and no bead lands whose only evidence is that nothing moved but colour.
